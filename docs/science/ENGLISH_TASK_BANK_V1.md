# ReadRight English Assessment Task Bank v1

Status: `UNVALIDATED_PILOT`
Version: `EN-TASKBANK-V1-PILOT-2026-09`

This bank is an original ReadRight pilot item bank. It does not copy DIBELS, DALI-DAB, NIPUN FLS, or any other standardized test items. The constructs are informed by established foundational-literacy assessment domains, while the exact ReadRight items, difficulty estimates, ordering, equivalence groups, and stopping thresholds remain unvalidated until expert review and field calibration are completed.

## Construct coverage

The v1 English bank is designed around 15 task families:

1. Oral language comprehension
2. Phoneme isolation
3. Phoneme blending
4. Phoneme segmentation
5. Phoneme manipulation, conditional only
6. Letter recognition
7. Grapheme-phoneme mapping
8. Phonological memory through nonword repetition
9. Rapid automatized naming
10. Real-word decoding
11. Nonword decoding
12. Word-reading automaticity
13. Oral reading fluency
14. Reading comprehension, literal and inferential
15. Spelling/encoding

Research basis for including these constructs includes the NIPUN Bharat Foundational Learning Study domains, the International Dyslexia Association's early screening guidance, and the component measures used by DIBELS 8. These sources support the constructs, not the validity of ReadRight's exact pilot items.

## Non-negotiable scientific rules

- A single failed item cannot establish a skill barrier.
- Negative evidence requires repeated usable observations from equivalent or parallel items.
- Prompted/modelled success is not evidence of independent mastery.
- Nonword reading is used to reduce reliance on memorized lexical forms, not to diagnose dyslexia.
- Rapid naming is interpreted only after the learner demonstrates knowledge of every symbol/object in the RAN form.
- Oral reading fluency must preserve accuracy, rate, error pattern, self-correction, and administration context separately.
- Reading comprehension must be interpreted alongside oral language and decoding evidence.
- Every pilot item remains `UNVALIDATED_PILOT` until psychometric calibration.
- The engine can abstain with `MORE_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED` or `CONFLICTING_EVIDENCE`.

## Calibration gates

Before any task family is promoted from pilot status, ReadRight must complete:

1. Content/linguistic expert review.
2. Bias and language-fairness review.
3. Cognitive interviews with children and teachers.
4. Inter-rater agreement analysis for human-scored responses.
5. Alternate-form and/or test-retest reliability where appropriate.
6. Item difficulty and discrimination analysis.
7. Differential item functioning or equivalent bias analysis with adequate samples.
8. External criterion validation against appropriate established measures and qualified literacy assessment.
9. Independent validation on schools/learners not used for calibration.

## Accuracy philosophy

ReadRight does not target a marketing claim of 100% diagnosis accuracy. It targets a very low confidently-wrong decision rate by allowing the engine to abstain, gather additional evidence, test prerequisites, and expose contradictions before an actionable barrier is inferred.

## Research references

- NIPUN Bharat Foundational Learning Study: oral language comprehension, phonological awareness, decoding, reading comprehension, and oral reading fluency with comprehension.
- International Dyslexia Association, Universal Screening K-2: phonological awareness, rapid automatic naming, letter-sound association, phonological memory, oral vocabulary, word recognition fluency, oral reading fluency, and reading comprehension by grade.
- DIBELS 8: letter naming, phonemic segmentation, nonsense-word decoding, word-reading fluency, oral-reading fluency, and Maze; technical documentation emphasizes ongoing validation.
- National Center on Intensive Intervention: classification accuracy, reliability, validity, statistical bias, and sample representativeness are distinct technical-quality dimensions.
